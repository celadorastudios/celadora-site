Accessibility

Last updated: 8 August 2026

We want everyone to be able to use what we build. Accessibility here is an ongoing effort rather than a finished state: we measure the site against WCAG 2.2 Level AA, work through what we find, and keep going. This page says where things stand and how to get help if something blocks you.

If any part of this site or our apps isn't accessible to you, tell us and we will get you the same information or function another way, at no cost to you, and work with you on a fix.

Email hello at celadora.net. We aim to acknowledge accessibility reports within five business days.

Where things stand

  • Contrast. The palette is built on a measured lightness scale rather than picked by eye. Body text sits well above the AA threshold against the dark background, and the purple accent clears AA for normal text (5.46:1 on the page background).
  • Keyboard. Everything reachable by mouse is reachable by keyboard, tab order follows the page, and interactive controls are real <button> and <a> elements rather than clickable divs. The consent banner can be dismissed with Escape.
  • Screen readers. Decorative graphics and icons are hidden from assistive technology so they aren't announced as meaningless nodes. Headings run in order, pages declare their language, and content sits in a <main> landmark.
  • Zoom and small screens. Type is set in relative units with fluid sizing, and layouts reflow rather than clipping.
  • No motion traps. Nothing autoplays, flashes or moves without you asking it to.
  • Nothing loads from someone else's CDN. The typeface, icons and scripts are served from this domain, so the site doesn't break when a content delivery network is blocked, slow or unreachable, a common failure mode on restricted networks and with content blockers. The privacy, terms and accessibility pages are additionally plain HTML and work with JavaScript switched off entirely.

What we're working on

Being specific is more useful than a blanket claim, so here is what is actively on the list:

  • A text equivalent for the product illustration. The menu-bar mockup on the home page (its chart, spend figures and insight cards) is a decorative picture built from sample data, and a screen reader gets no equivalent of the chart itself. The surrounding prose is written to carry the same information, and we're working on a proper text alternative.
  • Testing Claude-o-Meter with VoiceOver. It is a SwiftUI menu-bar app, so it inherits a reasonable baseline from Apple's controls, but we have not yet verified it, and its charts likely need the same text equivalent as the illustration above.
  • Reducing reliance on colour alone in the app. The Claude-o-Meter menu-bar readout turns red past your daily budget, and the history chart separates models by colour. We're adding a second, non-colour cue for both.

How we check

Assessment is our own: a mix of manual keyboard and screen-reader testing and measured contrast checks against the WCAG 2.2 AA thresholds. There has been no third-party audit, and self-assessment misses things, which is exactly why the offer at the top of this page matters more than the list above.

Tell us what's broken

A specific report ("the chart is unreadable in VoiceOver on macOS 15") is worth far more than a general one, and it goes to the front of the queue. Email hello at celadora.net, or message us through Ko-fi if you prefer. We'll tell you honestly what we can do and when, and in the meantime we'll get you the information or function another way.
