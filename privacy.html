Privacy policy

Last updated: 8 August 2026

Celadora Studios is a small independent studio, not a company with a data team. This policy is written to be read, not to be survived. It covers this website, celadora.net, and sets the baseline our apps follow.

The short version

  • There are no accounts here, and nothing to sign up for.
  • Cookie-based product analytics are off until you turn them on. Say no and PostHog is never contacted at all. A separate cookieless traffic counter (Cloudflare) does run on every visit and cannot identify you, details below.
  • We don't sell your personal information and we don't share it for advertising. The only companies that receive anything are the service providers listed below, and only so the site works.
  • Claude-o-Meter reads your Claude Code usage entirely on your own Mac. Your costs, projects and transcripts are never uploaded to us.

What this website collects

Analytics you have to agree to

PostHog is off until you say yes, and the banner on your first visit is the only thing that turns it on.

"Off" here means more than "sends no events". Until you agree, PostHog's script is never loaded onto the page at all, so your browser makes no connection to PostHog and they receive nothing about you, not even your IP address, which any request would otherwise reveal. Decline, and that stays true permanently.

If you do agree, PostHog records which pages you visit, which links and buttons you click, how long you stay, and basic performance timings. Being specific matters here: this is more than page views. Events carry no name, email or account, no profile is created for you (person_profiles: 'identified_only', and this site never calls identify), and session recording is disabled in code rather than merely promised. PostHog then stores a random identifier in your browser so one person reading three pages counts as one visit. PostHog Inc. processes this on our behalf on servers in the United States and receives your IP address as part of it.

Because we never identify you, these events are pseudonymous rather than anonymous. They include an IP address, which is personal data, so we treat them as personal data. In practice we have no way to connect an event to a person.

You can change your mind at any time using the link in the footer of every page. Withdrawing is as easy as agreeing, and your stored choice expires after twelve months so you get asked again.

Companies that see your visit simply because the page loads

Being honest about this matters more than looking clean. Loading a page makes your browser connect to these third parties, and a connection necessarily discloses your IP address and browser details to them:

Cookies and similar storage

The only thing this site stores on your device without asking is a single localStorage entry recording your analytics choice, including when that choice is "no", and the date you made it. It holds no identifier and exists so we don't ask you again. It is strictly necessary for honouring your own decision.

If you agree to analytics, PostHog additionally stores a random identifier (a cookie and a matching localStorage entry). There are no advertising, marketing or cross-site tracking cookies here.

Why we're allowed to do this (GDPR legal bases)

  • Consent (Article 6(1)(a)) for PostHog analytics. Freely given, and withdrawable at any time.
  • Legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)) for hosting, security and cookieless aggregate statistics. The interest is operating and maintaining a working website; the processing is limited to what serving a page requires. Fonts, icons and scripts are served from this domain, so they involve no third party at all.

You may object to processing based on legitimate interests at any time.

Where your data goes

Celadora Studios is in the United States, and the services above process data in the US. If you are in the EEA or UK, your data is transferred outside your home jurisdiction. Those transfers rely on the providers' standard contractual clauses and, where the provider is certified, the EU–US Data Privacy Framework. We hold no separate database of our own: everything described here lives inside the providers listed above. Ask us and we will tell you which safeguard applies to a specific transfer.

How long it's kept

We store none of it ourselves. We operate no database and keep no copies, so analytics data is kept for as long as our provider retains it (Cloudflare's schedule for traffic data, PostHog's for events) and no longer. We don't archive, export or extend it. Your analytics choice is stored on your own device for twelve months, then expires and you're asked again.

Our apps

Claude-o-Meter

Claude-o-Meter runs entirely on your Mac. It reads the usage logs Claude Code already writes to ~/.claude/projects/**/*.jsonl, aggregates them locally, and stores its own state and pricing table in ~/Library/Application Support/. It contains no analytics, no telemetry and no crash reporting. Your spend figures, project names and transcript contents never leave your machine and are never transmitted to Celadora Studios.

One exception, stated plainly: on launch, and when you ask it to, the app contacts api.github.com to check whether a newer release exists, and downloads from GitHub if you choose to update. That request tells GitHub your IP address and the app version you are running. It sends nothing about your usage or spend. The app is MIT-licensed, so you can build it yourself with that check removed.

partsbin

partsbin keeps your designs in your own browser's local storage and has no server-side save. It carries its own privacy policy inside the app, which governs it in detail.

Donations

Ko-fi handles any donation end to end. We never see your card details. That transaction is covered by Ko-fi's privacy policy, not this one.

Children

These are developer tools and are not directed at children under 13 (or under 16 in the EEA). We do not knowingly collect personal data from them.
